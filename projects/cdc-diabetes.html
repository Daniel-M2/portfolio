Background

A rising trend that hasn't leveled off

Diabetes prevalence in the U.S. climbed from 10.3% to 13.2% between 2001 and 2020, and the trend hasn't slowed. This project looks at the lab, lifestyle, and demographic variables behind that rise, aiming at two concrete targets: a 5% cut in prevalence and a 15% cut in incidence within five years.

Dataset

CDC Diabetes Health Indicators

Survey-based health indicator data covering lab results, lifestyle habits, income, education, and demographics, used as the basis for both descriptive and inferential analysis.

Methodology

Descriptive & inferential analysis

The core approach combined descriptive statistics with inferential testing: normality testing, outlier analysis, correlation testing via Chi-square, and independent t-tests to confirm which relationships in the data were statistically meaningful rather than noise.

Results

Who's most at risk, and why

Diabetes prevalence rate

13,000+Diagnosed per 100,000 people
71.69%Of diagnosed cases fall in the 55–79 age group
  • The 55–79 age group is the most prevalent, with rates ranging from 10.21% to 18.71%

Age & gender

  • At age 35+, diabetes risk is more than 2× that of people in their 20s
  • Males are 17% more likely to have diabetes than females

Income & education

Higher education and income levels reduce diabetes risk by more than 60% — one of the strongest protective factors in the dataset.

Diet, alcohol, smoking & exercise

  • Eating at least 1 fruit and 1 vegetable per day cuts risk by 18% and 29% respectively
  • Alcohol consumption showed an anomalous pattern, likely due to imbalanced sample groups with pre-existing poor health records
  • Active smokers are 37% more likely to have diabetes
  • Regular physical exercise lowers the probability of diabetes by close to 50%

Conclusions

What actually moves the needle

For the general public, the data points to a short list of high-leverage habits for reducing diabetes risk:

  • Eat at least 1 fruit and 1 vegetable a day
  • Exercise at least twice a week
  • Join a regular sports community — tennis, paddle, pilates, gym
  • Get a medical check-up twice a year from age 30 onward
  • Stop smoking

Business Recommendations

Where the opportunity is

Increase insulin & blood sugar test supply

With diabetes trending upward, pharmaceutical suppliers need to keep test kits in stock to meet rising demand.

Develop tasty sugar-free meal plans

Catering businesses can build healthier, better-tasting menus and partner with gyms, pilates studios, hospitals, and clinics.

Promote sugar substitutes

Products like stevia give sweet-tooth patients a realistic path to a low-sugar diet without feeling deprived.

Reassess insurance policy pricing

As the diabetes risk trend continues to climb, insurers may need to adjust policy terms for diabetic individuals to maintain profitability.
