Background

Beauty standards, and the bias baked into them

Most beauty clinics and skincare brands calibrate their standards toward one skin tone or ethnicity, usually whichever is dominant in the brand's origin market — in Asia, that's typically the South Korean beauty standard. That doesn't map well onto people with different skin tones, including much of South and Southeast Asia. Skin-check prediction tools built on that same bias end up giving inaccurate, sometimes misleading results to a large share of their users.

Dataset

~20,000 labeled faces, three age classes

19,906Labeled face images
3Age classes: Young, Middle, Old

Sourced from Kaggle, with no missing values but a noticeably imbalanced class distribution across the three age groups — a factor that shaped both the modeling approach and how the results should be read.

Methodology

Custom CNN vs. pre-trained architectures

Multiple CNN architectures were built and evaluated — both hand-built and pre-trained — varying hidden layers, dropout rates, and epochs, and comparing them on loss and accuracy. Xception and EfficientNetB2 were used as the pre-trained baselines against the custom model.

Results

Consistent across skin tones, weaker on "Old"

68–71%Model accuracy across age classes
  • The YOUNG class images showed varied skin tones, a handful of grayscale photos, some closed-eye and half-profile shots — realistic, messy real-world data
  • The MIDDLE class included a high share of facial hair (22/40 sampled images), which the model had to learn to look past
  • The OLD class included a high share of eyeglasses (18/40), adding another visual confound
  • Image sizes and formats varied throughout, requiring resizing before training

Conclusions

Good enough to ship as a prototype

The model struggles most with the OLD class, but since most beauty-clinic clients skew younger, that's a manageable limitation rather than a blocker. More importantly, the 68–71% accuracy holds consistently across pale, light, dark, and even grayscale photos — meaning the model isn't quietly biased toward one skin tone, which was the whole point of building it.

Business Recommendations

Where this fits in a real product

Prototype age recognition

The model is ready to be tested as an early-stage prototype for age detection inside beauty clinic intake tools.

Inclusivity as a trust signal

Low bias across skin tones and ethnicities can be marketed directly — it builds client trust and brand reputation in diverse markets.
